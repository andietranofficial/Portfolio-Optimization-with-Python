How Optimo works

Optimo tests thousands of ways to split your money across a set of stocks, then shows the split that best fits your goal and how it compares with simply splitting equally.

Workflow

  1. Pick your stocks. Add 2 to 10 tickers you would actually hold. Optimo only changes the weights; it never adds new names.
  2. Set the look-back period. Daily prices from this window feed every number. A longer period smooths out short bursts but can include older market conditions.
  3. Set your assumptions. Investment amount, risk-free rate (today's safe return, used in Sharpe) and the maximum weight per stock, which stops the result piling into one name.
  4. Choose a goal. Best risk-adjusted return picks the highest Sharpe ratio. Best return within a risk limit picks the highest return whose volatility stays at or below your limit.
  5. Run the optimisation. Optimo creates 10,000 random weightings that each add up to 100%, scores them all, and picks the winner.
  6. Review, then save or export. Compare with equal weight, click other dots to explore alternatives, then save the run or copy the weights.

Reading the results

Headline cards
Return, volatility, Sharpe and today's value for the chosen portfolio. The line underneath shows the gap to equal weight: green is better, red is worse. For volatility, lower is better.
Efficient frontier
Each dot is one tested portfolio. Higher means more return, further right means more risk, darker green means a better Sharpe. The strongest options sit on the upper-left edge. The orange dot is your result; the black diamond is equal weight.
Allocation
How much of your money goes into each stock, in percent, dollars and approximate shares, plus the shift versus equal weight.
Growth of your investment
What the amount would have become if you had bought these weights on the first day and held them, next to equal weight and the S&P 500. Look at the dips as well as the end value.
Forward test
Optimo picks weights using only the earlier data, then checks them on the last 3, 6 or 12 months it never saw. If they still beat equal weight there, the result is more trustworthy. Weight stability shows how much the weights move between the two runs.
Correlation
How closely stocks move together. Pairs near 1 offer little diversification; low or negative pairs are what lets the optimiser cut risk.
Per-stock metrics
Each stock on its own. A stock with a weak Sharpe often ends up with a small weight, unless it diversifies the rest.

Rules of thumb

  • Sharpe above 1 is strong, 0.5 to 1 is reasonable, below 0.5 is a weak reward for the risk.
  • A near-zero weight means the stock added risk without enough return over this period, not that it is a bad company.
  • Re-run with a different look-back period. If the weights swing a lot, treat the result as fragile.
  • Results describe the past. They are a starting point for discussion, not a forecast.

Method

10,000 random weightings, each summing to 100% with none above your per-stock limit, are scored on annualised return, volatility and Sharpe using 252 trading days. Prices are daily adjusted closes from Yahoo Finance (simulated in this mockup).

Optimo is an analysis tool and does not give investment advice.
